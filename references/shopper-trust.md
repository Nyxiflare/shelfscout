# Trust signals by marketplace

Check the current page: marketplaces change their labels and policies often. These notes are what to
*look for*, not guarantees of what you'll find.

## Amazon
- **Who sells and ships it.** "Ships from / Sold by Amazon" or the brand's own store is the safest.
  "Sold by [unknown seller], fulfilled by Amazon" is usually fine for delivery, but counterfeit risk
  for branded goods sits with the seller.
- **Seller profile:** % positive over the last 12 months, number of ratings, and how long they've been
  selling.
- **Review merges:** variations (colours, sizes, even different products) can share one review pool.
  Check that the reviews describe *this* item.
- "Amazon's Choice" and "Best Seller" badges reflect sales and relevance, not quality.
- Prices for the same item differ between variations and sellers. Check "Other sellers on Amazon".

## eBay
- **Seller feedback %** and total feedback count, "Top Rated" status, and item location (shipping time
  and import charges).
- Condition labels ("new", "open box", "refurbished") and the seller's return policy, which varies per
  listing.
- eBay Money Back Guarantee covers most purchases. Payment outside eBay isn't covered.

## Etsy
- Handmade vs. "designed by / produced with a partner" (many listings are print-on-demand or resold).
- Star Seller badge, shop sales count, and how long the shop has been open. Reviews with photos are the
  strongest evidence.
- Processing time (before shipping) plus shipping time, which is often longer than people expect.

## Walmart
- "Sold and shipped by Walmart" vs. marketplace sellers. Check the seller name and its rating.

## AliExpress / Temu / similar cross-border
- Delivery estimates of 1–4 weeks, and **import duty and tax** for the buyer's country. Use `SS landed`
  with an estimated `--duty-pct`, and say it's an estimate.
- Store rating, years open, and "Choice"/local-warehouse labels that change delivery time.
- Spec inflation (storage capacity, battery mAh, wattage) is common on cheap electronics. Treat
  impossible specs as a red flag.
- Buyer protection depends on paying on-platform and opening disputes within the platform's window.

## Universal red flags
- A price far below every other seller for a branded item → counterfeit or scam risk.
- "No returns" on items that commonly fail or need sizing.
- A seller asking to pay or talk off-platform.
- A sale price that was never the real price before (`SS deal`).
- Reviews describing a different product, or a burst of near-identical 5-star reviews (`SS reviews`).
- Brand names that are random letters on a product that's also sold under many other names: usually the
  same factory item. Fine to buy, but pick the trustworthy seller and the lowest total cost.
